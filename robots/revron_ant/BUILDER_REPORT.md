# Revron-style antweight vertical spinner: builder report

## 1. Final status
- Spec: /tmp/builder1/spec.json (generated by /tmp/builder1/gen.py from template.json). Export: /tmp/builder1/out/revron_ant (47 files).
- Verify result: 0 blockers, 3 warnings, ok=true. Mass is 130.4 g nominal (range 110.1 to 150.7 g). Footprint is a 127 mm square.
- Design: the template's 2WD nylon shells and N10 drive are kept. A new `frame` (nylon structure) has two side arms and a top flange, held by the front shell bolts, lengthened to M2x18. A 40 mm titanium disc (2.5 mm thick, two teeth, 46 mm tip diameter) sits direct-drive on an 1806 2300 kV outrunner, with the stator bolted to the right arm by 2x M2. A 12 A brushless ESC sits in a new pocket. The nylon wedge has a slot so the disc passes through it. The steel locking pin goes through the left arm into a hole in the disc. Phase wires run through a slot in the arm and a hole in the roof.
- Tip speed (my own calculation, because the tool reports none): 2300 kV x 8.4 V is about 19,300 rpm. At r = 23 mm that is about 47 m/s, or about 104 mph, well under 600.
- Remaining warnings:
  - `w_sw_wesc` bend radius. It moved around through 5 rounds of widening the channel and never went away. I think it comes from the grid router's 45-degree turns, not from the geometry.
  - mass hi bound of 150.7 g. This is catalog uncertainty, and there are no real parts to weigh.
  - wedge_load 39%. This is normal for a front-heavy vertical spinner riding on its forks. I accepted it.

## 2. Friction log (ordered by cost)
1. **The wire router only searches inside `structure` parts. Nothing says so.**
   - The weapon motor sat between arms that were part of the `wedge` part. For 6 rounds the router "got within 31 mm" and blamed `chassis_top` or `wedge` in turn at the same point.
   - I only found the cause by changing the wedge's kind to structure as an experiment.
   - Fix: write the rule in the guide, and make the error say "the end port is outside the routable (structure) volume".
2. **The default `solid_fraction` is not 0.45, although the guide says it is.**
   - Setting 0.45 explicitly on the shells dropped the mass from 153.7 to 130.9 g.
   - Leaving it unset looks like about 0.85 or more for structure parts. That cost me 3 rounds chasing mass.
3. **`mass_g` on a made part is ignored without any warning.**
4. **Verify gives no mass breakdown per part.** I had to find each part's mass by setting its solid_fraction to 0.0001 and comparing totals.
5. **Port positions are not documented.** `catalog` lists port names, not where they sit on the part. I had to infer that `weapon_motor.phases` is on the +local-z face from the "gets within N mm" distances.
6. **Orientation of catalog parts is not documented.** The guide doesn't say which axis the outrunner's shaft runs along, so I guessed z and rotated it [0,90,0].
7. **`spin_axis` must be a 3-element array.** The guide says "(local axis)". I tried "z" and got a serde error at a column number.
8. **Wire bend warnings don't respond to `slack_mm`.** Widening a channel only moves the warning to another corner. There is no way to give a route by hand (waypoints).
9. **One trapped part produces a wall of errors.** The 14-blocker list was almost all knock-on effects of one bolt head that overlapped. The root cause should be listed first.
10. **The `section_x` and `section_z` views can't take a cut position.**
11. **`render` usage doesn't list the views.**

## 3. Verifier gaps (wrong or unrealistic, NOT flagged), most serious first
1. **No weapon or rules checks run at all.** As a test I removed `lock_pin`, set `locking_bar: null`, set reduction 0.1 (a 10x overspeed, over 1000 mph) and set `weapon_remote_disable: false`. Verify still said 0 blockers, ok=true. Despite the guide, nothing checks tip speed, the locking bar, the failsafe, remote disable, the switch or sharp edges.
2. **Mass over 150 g is only a warning.** At 153.7 g nominal (before the solid_fraction fix) it was ok=true. A false claim is also only a warning.
3. **Weapon retention is unchecked.**
   - The disc is held to the outrunner bell by a `connections` entry that says "screw" with a reason. No real fastener exists and none was required: the screw check only fires on pairs that involve structure.
   - The motor-to-arm screws use `threads_into: weapon_motor`, which the guide doesn't allow. That was accepted too.
4. **No bearing or support model.**
   - The disc is cantilevered off a single 1806 bell bearing, with no outboard support. The left arm only carries the lock pin.
   - The full side load of a hit goes into a hobby motor's bearings. Revron supports both sides.
   - I tried a dead shaft through both arms. It was flagged only as an overlap with the motor, not as a design question.
5. **The arms are weak.**
   - Each arm is a 4 mm nylon plate on a 4x6.8 mm bridge to a 2 mm flange. It is cantilevered about 35 mm forward and carries the whole weapon reaction.
   - There is no stress, stiffness or impact check. The arms float above the wedge with nothing tying them to it.
6. **The phase wires run exposed.** They cross the outer top of the frame and the outside of the arm, next to the spinning disc and in the line of hits. Being "inside the routable volume" includes the open air between the arms. There is no wire-to-weapon clearance check.
7. **The disc reaches past the wedge tip (y 87 vs 82).** Disc ground clearance is about 1.5 mm, and teeth can strike the floor when the robot pitches. There is no check of dynamic pitch or floor strikes.
8. **Gyro effects and spin-up are not modelled.** There is no energy (J), spin-up time, or ESC current against the motor. A 12 A ESC on an 1806 is fine, but nothing checks that.
9. **Running inverted.** `can_run_inverted: false` was accepted silently. A vertical spinner gets flipped often. The arms stick up 23 mm, about 9 mm above the wheel tops, so it can't drive upside down. That deserves at least a warning.
10. **Materials.**
    - The titanium disc is fine, but the tool would also accept a nylon or PLA disc unless brittleness is checked; I didn't test that.
    - The nylon wedge or fork takes hits and is 1.2 mm ribbed. No impact-thickness check.
11. **Battery access and the tape-retained ESC.** Opening the battery means removing 4 shell bolts and the frame. Nothing checks serviceability.
12. **Switch and lock pin.** The power switch position is unchanged from the template. Nothing checks how easy the lock pin is to reach, or that it actually engages the disc hole: a 1.7 mm hole for a 1.5 mm pin was inferred, never verified.
13. **Disc hole and pin clearance was not checked.** The spin check passed with the pin sitting inside the disc hole, so `interference.spin` seems to ignore the locking bar. That is right while it is engaged, but there is no "pin removed" state.

## 4. False alarms
- **The bend warnings are partly artefacts of the router.** 45-degree turns at corners of a 3+ mm-wide channel would bend fine with real wire slack. A template wire (`w_sw_esc`) began to warn after I added unrelated wires nearby.
- **wedge_load 39%** is a fair observation, but for a vertical spinner, weight on the forks is usually intended.
- The first-round overlap and spin errors were all genuine.

## 5. What I wanted to express but couldn't
- **Bolting the disc to the bell:** fasteners into a bought part with a bolt circle (catalog mount holes for the outrunner stator and bell).
- **Bearings and shafts:** a bearing in the arm (there is no catalog bearing) and a shaft that legitimately passes through the motor bore (`allow_overlap` format undocumented).
- **Wires:** fixed waypoints, or cable ties and clips.
- **Weapon physics:** spin-up time, energy, and weapon mass versus motor torque.
- **Mass:** a per-part mass report, and real slicer infill per region (dense skin with sparse bosses) instead of one solid_fraction.
- **States:** the lock pin as removable (a "pin out" state), and a motor ↔ phases port side choice.

## Honesty notes
- I set shell `solid_fraction` to 0.45, the value the guide calls the default, to get the mass under the limit. Without it the tool computes about 153 g.
- The disc-to-bell "screw" connection is declared only. No real fasteners are modelled.
